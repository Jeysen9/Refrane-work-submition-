# PART II — PREMIUM VISUAL DESIGN

## 5. GLASSMORPHISM MUST BE A DEFINING VISUAL ELEMENT

I want the entire website to use premium glass-style cards and surfaces.

Glassmorphism should be part of the RecruitFlow design identity.

However, it must be executed at a very high standard.

Do not simply apply `backdrop-filter: blur()` to every rectangle and call the design finished.

The cards should feel like beautifully crafted translucent glass panels arranged in a sophisticated, layered environment.

### Glass card characteristics

Use combinations of:

- Translucent backgrounds.
- Carefully controlled backdrop blur.
- Fine semi-transparent borders.
- Subtle internal highlights.
- Soft realistic shadows.
- Delicate reflections.
- Layered depth.
- Gentle light gradients.
- Appropriate background separation.
- Consistent corner radii.

Suggested starting values:

- Dark glass surfaces: translucent dark navy or charcoal.
- Light glass surfaces: low-opacity white.
- Border opacity: subtle and context-sensitive.
- Blur: approximately 12–24px where appropriate.
- Corner radii: consistent, refined, and modern.
- Shadows: soft and multidimensional rather than harsh.

These are starting points, not fixed requirements.

Adjust the implementation to suit each background and ensure sufficient text contrast.

### Different types of glass cards

Create a reusable design system for:

**Feature cards**

Refined translucent cards containing clear icons, concise headings, and short descriptions.

**Statistics cards**

Beautifully arranged numbers with carefully chosen accent colours and restrained graphical details.

**Interface preview cards**

Glass-framed previews of the RecruitFlow application.

**Pricing cards**

Premium glass panels with clear pricing hierarchy and excellent readability.

**Testimonial cards**

Elegant glass containers that present genuine customer quotes without excessive decoration.

**Interactive cards**

Cards that respond to hover or click with subtle changes in lighting, border, shadow, or perspective.

### Glass-card hover interactions

On desktop, interactive glass cards may respond with:

- A slight lift.
- A subtle change in surface lighting.
- A refined border highlight.
- A small perspective shift.
- Gentle elevation.
- A smooth transition back to the resting state.

Use restraint.

Do not make every card tilt dramatically or bounce.

### Important readability rule

**Glassmorphism must never reduce legibility.**

Text must remain clearly readable regardless of the image, animation, or gradient behind a glass panel.

Increase surface opacity where necessary.

Do not place white text on transparent glass over a bright image.

The website should feel elegant, not visually confusing.

- Layered foreground and background movement.

### Essential transition rule

Every important section should have an intentional relationship with the section before and after it.

1. What is leaving the screen?
2. What remains visible?
3. What is moving into the scene?
4. How does the background change?
5. Which object guides the user's attention?
6. How is depth communicated?
7. How does the scene settle into a readable state?

Treat the website as a visual story.

### Example transition: Testimonials to Final CTA

The final action should feel like the natural conclusion of the story.

### Important motion restrictions

Do not:

- Use the same fade-up animation for everything.

- Make sections feel disconnected.

- Force unusual scroll behaviour unnecessarily.
- Allow pinned sections to trap users.
- Create large blank spaces between scenes.
- Introduce abrupt background changes without intention.
- Let elements move across text and obstruct readability.

Scroll progression must remain smooth and intuitive.
